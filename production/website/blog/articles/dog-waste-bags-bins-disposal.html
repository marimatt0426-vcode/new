After You Scoop: Dog Waste Bags, Bins, and Disposal

Yard Care
September 12, 2026•2 min read

Picking up dog waste is only half the routine. You also need a reliable way to contain it, store it briefly, and put it into an accepted disposal stream. A simple setup makes it easier to finish the job every time.

Keep bags where you will use them

Store a supply near the door or with your scoop, out of reach of pets and children. Check that bags are intact and large enough to close securely around the amount being collected.

Tie or seal each bag before placing it in the appropriate waste container. Avoid leaving filled bags on the lawn, beside a path, or balanced on a fence with the intention of collecting them later. That creates another task someone has to remember.

Choose a practical bin location

A closed outdoor container can provide a designated holding place between pickup and collection. Keep it stable and away from places where bags could fall into drains or be scattered.

Choose a location that does not block the gate, a walkway, or the service entrance. Empty and maintain the container as part of the routine, following your collection provider’s requirements.

If you share a property, agree on who handles the bin. A bag dispenser does not help much when the adjoining container remains full.

Follow your actual collection rules

Local collection programs and private haulers can have different requirements for bagging and containers. Check the instructions for the address where you live. Indianapolis residents can start with the city’s trash pickup information; households elsewhere should use their own municipality or hauler.

Do not assume dog waste belongs in a leaf bag, recycling cart, or general backyard compost pile. The EPA lists pet waste among the materials to avoid in ordinary backyard composting. A bag labeled compostable does not, by itself, make its contents acceptable to a compost program.

Keep waste out of drains

Do not wash dog waste into a street, gutter, or storm drain. The EPA explains that stormwater can carry pet waste from the ground into streams, lakes, and rivers. Collection and proper containment help keep it out of that route. See the EPA’s pet waste management guidance.

After handling waste or cleanup equipment, wash your hands and store the tools separately from food and household-use items.

Clarify disposal when hiring a service

Ask whether the provider removes collected waste or leaves bagged material in your bin. Those are different service arrangements.
