Weekly vs. Twice-Weekly Dog Poop Cleanup: Which Fits Your Yard?

Service Guides
September 20, 2026•2 min read

The right cleanup frequency is the one that leaves your household comfortable using the yard between visits. Weekly and twice-weekly plans both provide a recurring routine, but they create different gaps between cleanups and different total costs. Neither plan stops new waste from appearing after the team leaves.

Start with how your dogs and your household actually use the outdoor space, then compare the same scope at each frequency.

Look at the days between cleanups

A weekly visit gives the yard one scheduled cleanup each week. Twice-weekly service divides the work across two visits. The practical difference is how much time passes before waste that appears after one visit is picked up at the next.

Think about when the yard feels ready to use. Is a quick family pickup between weekly visits easy to manage? Or does buildup make the lawn less inviting well before the next visit? A smaller heavily used section may feel busy even when the property itself is large.

Dog count matters, but it is not the entire decision. Two dogs using a dedicated run create a different situation from two dogs spreading their time across several yard sections. Count the actual cleanup area consistently when comparing plans.

Compare the weekly total, not just the visit price

Purge Pros charges per visit. For the starting example of one dog, up to one-eighth acre and one service area, weekly service starts at $19.99 per visit. The equivalent twice-weekly starting rate is $15.99 per visit. Two $15.99 visits total $31.98 in a typical week with two visits.

Keep the initial cleanup separate

If waste has built up, distinguish that first cleanup from the ongoing maintenance rhythm. Eligible new recurring customers receive their entire first cleanup at their approved recurring visit price, with the initial scoop surcharge waived. The first visit is paid, and eligibility and household history still matter. One-time cleanups use separate terms.

Frequency should be chosen for the routine you want afterward, rather than just the first visit. Read the current service terms before agreeing to a plan.
