Should You Pick Up Dog Poop Before Mowing?

Yard Care
September 12, 2026•2 min read

Yes—pick up visible dog waste before mowing. A mower can spread or smear waste rather than remove it, making the lawn and equipment more unpleasant to deal with afterward. The simplest order is pickup, a final check, then mowing.

Start before the mower is brought out

Walk the lawn with a scoop and suitable bags. Work in strips so you can keep track of where you have already checked, then inspect the fence edges and your dog’s favorite spots separately.

Pick up toys, sticks, and other small objects as a separate preparation step. If the grass is long, take extra time to inspect it from more than one direction. Some waste may remain hidden until the area is easier to see.

Keep the two jobs distinct

Dog waste removal and lawn mowing are different services. A mowing company may require the lawn to be free of pet waste before it starts. A scoop service may need the grass and leaves maintained enough to inspect the ground.

Ask each provider what preparation it expects. Do not assume either company will perform the other’s job or that one appointment automatically covers both.

If heavy leaves are involved, use our fall cleanup sequence to coordinate visible pickup, leaf removal, and another check.

Coordinate dates with room for change

If you use two providers, request pickup before the usual mowing visit where schedules allow. Ask what service days are available rather than promising the lawn crew an exact scoop arrival time.

Weather and route changes can disrupt the order. Keep the relevant contact information handy and confirm updates. A quote request does not reserve a specific appointment.

Even after a professional pickup, dogs may use the lawn again before mowing. A short final inspection is still useful, especially if several hours or a day have passed.

If the mower encounters waste

Stop and handle the cleanup according to the mower manufacturer’s safety and cleaning instructions. Do not reach near moving parts or improvise a method that could start the equipment unexpectedly.

Collect remaining visible waste from the yard before continuing. Keep waste and dirty cleaning water out of drains, and wash your hands after handling the cleanup.

Use the freshly cut lawn for a follow-up look

Shorter grass can expose areas that were difficult to inspect earlier. Check those spots and note any recurring blind areas, such as the base of shrubs or a narrow fence line.

This is also a useful time to review the service area. If your dogs have started using another section, tell the provider before the next visit.
