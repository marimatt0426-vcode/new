Adding a Second Dog? Recheck Your Yard Cleanup Plan

Service Guides
September 20, 2026•2 min read

A new dog can change more than the number on a service form. The household may open a second yard section, create a separate run or spend more time outside. Before the next cleanup, make sure the service team knows what changed.

An existing account does not automatically update itself when another dog arrives. A brief scope review helps keep the work, price and pet arrangement aligned.

Update the actual dog count

Tell the team the current number of dogs using the property and when the change takes effect. If another dog is visiting temporarily, explain the duration rather than silently treating a short stay as a permanent plan change. Ask the team how that situation should be handled.

Do not continue using an old one-dog price as proof of the rate for a changed household. Purge Pros’ standard pricing considers dog count along with frequency, cleanup area and selected sections. The multiple-dog guide explains how those inputs fit together.

Check whether the service area changed

Introducing another dog sometimes means opening a side yard or separating pets into different spaces. List the sections that need pickup now and distinguish them from areas still excluded. A dog run behind an interior gate should not disappear from the request simply because it is on the same lot.

Use the yard-size guide if you need to reassess the area. Estimate the outdoor space to be searched, not the full parcel including the house.

Revisit the routine between visits

Watch how quickly the used sections become uncomfortable for your household. Some families continue with the same visit frequency and handle occasional pickup themselves. Others want a more frequent recurring routine.

Compare the full cost of the new plan. Per-visit billing means two visits in a week cost the sum of those two visits. A lower individual visit rate at a different frequency is not automatically a lower weekly bill. Confirm any requested schedule change with the team.

Review access and pet arrangements

A pet arrangement that worked for one dog may need a different approach with two. Explain where the dogs will be during entry and service, including any interior gates or separate runs. The goal is a practical arrangement the household and team have agreed to follow.

Keep access details in the established service conversation. Do not submit a duplicate new-customer request just to tell the team that dog count changed. A new dog at the same household also does not automatically create eligibility for another introductory offer.

New customers can review dog waste removal and check a price using the current dog count. Existing customers should ask the team to confirm the updated scope, rate and timing before assuming the change is in effect.
