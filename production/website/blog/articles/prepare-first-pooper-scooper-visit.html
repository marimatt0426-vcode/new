How to Prepare for Your First Pooper Scooper Visit

Service Guides
September 12, 2026•3 min read

Your first pooper scooper visit goes more smoothly when the service team can identify the right yard, enter the agreed areas, and understand any pet or property instructions. You do not need to make the yard look perfect before asking for help. You do need a clear plan for access and scope.

Use this checklist after your service details and proposed day have been agreed.

Confirm what is being cleaned

Review the address, dog count, yard-size range, and selected front, back, or side areas. Include a separate run, patio, or other section where pickup is needed. If one area is excluded, say so directly.

Describe current buildup honestly, including anything hidden by tall grass or leaves. This helps set expectations about visibility and the work involved. If you do not know when the yard was last cleaned, saying that is more useful than guessing.

Check which first-visit terms apply. Eligible new recurring customers pay the approved regular visit rate for their entire first cleanup under the current surcharge-waiver offer. One-time cleanup and returning-customer situations follow different rules. The service options and terms explain those distinctions.

Make the access route usable

Walk from the street or driveway to the service gate. Move anything that blocks the agreed entry, such as stacked furniture, a vehicle, bins, or a tangled hose. Check that the gate opens and closes normally.

Share a sticky latch, an unusual gate location, or any access arrangement through the agreed private communication channel. Avoid relying on an old instruction that no longer works. If there are multiple gates, identify the one the team should use and any that should remain untouched.

Our gate-access checklist covers the details worth communicating before service day.

Arrange where the dogs will be

Agree on the pet arrangement with the team in advance. Keeping dogs in a separate secure space during access can prevent an unexpected encounter at the gate. Do not assume a dog that is relaxed with family will respond the same way when a service provider enters.

Share relevant access concerns, and tell other household members when the visit is expected. A gate propped open by someone else or a dog released into the yard can change the plan even if your original instructions were clear.

Clear small obstacles without doing the whole cleanup

Pick up loose toys, empty bowls, and items that make searching the lawn difficult. Identify sprinkler heads, fragile decorations, and areas with unfinished yard work. For heavy leaves or vegetation, discuss whether separate yard work is needed to make waste visible.

You do not need to perform the paid cleanup yourself. The purpose of preparation is to make the agreed space accessible and understandable, not to erase the reason you requested service.

Know what happens next

An online price check or request does not itself confirm a booking or charge a card. Purge Pros reviews route availability before final scheduling and secure payment setup. Keep your preferred contact details current so that questions or changes reach you.

Before the first visit, keep a short note with the agreed scope, access arrangement, pet plan, and billing terms. Afterward, review the completion communication and raise any question while the details are fresh. Future visits become simpler when the first one establishes a clear routine.

If you are still choosing a plan, check your ZIP and price and use this checklist to prepare an accurate request.
