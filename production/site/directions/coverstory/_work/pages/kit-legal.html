01

A first example clause

A paragraph of the document, in a reading measure of about 70 characters. Running text of the live document goes here, word for word, set by the script and never typed.

  • A label: The sentence that belongs to it, run in exactly as the document has it.
  • A fee or a duty: A list in a legal document states costs and obligations as well as benefits, so the marker is a quiet dot, never a tick.
02

A second example clause & a longer name

Each clause keeps the anchor the live page uses, so a link from an email or a message still lands on it. The script binds an ampersand in a heading to the word before it, so no line starts with it; the file keeps the plain space of the live text.

Contact

L2 On this pagenav.legal-toc[data-legal-toc] · p.legal-toc__label · .legal-toc__body > ol.legal-toc__list · button.legal-toc__print[data-legal-print][hidden]One jump link per clause, in the words of its heading; the two-digit numbers are drawn by a counter, so the link text is the heading and nothing else. Every link is a 44px target. Without the script it is the plain card shown above at every width. With js/family-legal.js: the link of the clause being read is marked (aria-current, the gold row; on a desktop the clause whose heading has reached the middle of the window, and a clause that was jumped to stays marked while the page rests there). From 900px “Print this page” sits in the label row; it follows the list in the markup, as on the live page. In a short desktop window with a mouse (under 840px tall) the rows close up to a 36px pitch so fourteen fit at 1366 by 768 and 1280 by 720; if a list still does not fit, it scrolls inside the card and ends on a whole row. Under 900px the card becomes a slim bar pinned under the header (.legal-toc--js, button.legal-toc__bar): it wears the icon of the clause being read and its name on up to two lines, a band in the ground colour of the section lies behind it so no line of the document shows above or beside it, and it opens the list, which stays above the pinned price bar and ends on a whole row. Narrow this window to see the bar. The band reads the section’s --sec-bg; outside a .sec set --legal-ground on .legal-doc, as this page does.

L9 The date line of the document.sh.sh--center > … h2.sh__title + p.legal-updated > span.legal-updated__s (one per sentence)The short date line is in the hero (hero.date, a shared piece), on the first screen at every size. This is the document’s own line, in full, as the last line of the heading block of the document section, outside the sheet. While approved text is not yet published it says so in two sentences, which the hero’s line cannot hold (60 characters). Each sentence is a span that does not break inside, so on a phone the line breaks between the two; the date is bound with no-break spaces. Once the page carries a publication date (“Last updated: …” in the hero) this line is not written. Both lines print: the hero’s under the title, this one over the document.

L8 Print@media printPrinting a page that holds a .legal-doc gives the document alone: title, date, summary, every clause and the mailing address in ink on white, in a measure of six inches. Text flows from page to page: a clause is not held whole on one page, only its heading stays with its first lines and a list item is not split. The hero’s date line prints under the title as a plain line, and the document’s full date line over the document. The header, the hero picture, the facts strip, the list of links, the photograph, the buttons of the closing ask, the mascot line, the closing scene and the footer are left out. Nothing changes on screen.
